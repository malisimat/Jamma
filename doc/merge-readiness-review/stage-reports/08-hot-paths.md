## System understanding

The branch's high-level real-time design is good in several important respects. Timing decisions are made off the audio thread and published through a fixed latest-wins command. The audio callback consumes at most one command at its block boundary, applies Timer and entity changes before ordinary advancement, and performs no diagnostic I/O itself. NINJAM scratch and pointer arrays are prepared by `SetAudioFormat`; `ProcessExportBlock` skips rather than resizing when a callback exceeds prepared capacity. The per-loop correction and restore functions are `noexcept` and use fixed work proportional to the live hierarchy.

Four boundaries remain incomplete. First, current live timing bypasses the mailbox/snapshot boundary and calls several opaque NJClient getters from the callback, even though the bundled NJClient interface reserves that thread for `AudioProc`. Second, the large Station diagnostic routine is entered unconditionally from callback-reachable record-completion actions and performs unbounded allocation and console I/O when verbose mode is enabled. Third, the common remote-to-local elapsed calculation is still copied into every take, so the active map pays the same division repeatedly every block before wrapping each entity by its own length. Fourth, Stage 7's empty-scene reset handoff proves that a transition in `Scene::OnTick` can clear job-owned containers and traverse raw station/take state on the callback.

The third point does not challenge the protected timing model. There must be one common mapped source coordinate and distinct per-entity anchors/moduli. Computing that common coordinate once in `AudioHost` and passing it down removes repeated common work while preserving every loop's distinct cursor.

## Candidate findings

### S08-01 — Keep opaque NJClient getters and repeated session acquisition out of the audio callback

- Stage / reviewer: Stage 8 — Audio and other hot-path performance.
- Scope reviewed / exclusions: callback call chain and opaque-call cost; Stage 7 owns whether the same calls race and whether the current connection-use protocol is memory-safe; Stage 12 owns disconnect/destruction ordering.
- Severity: must fix before merge.
- Evidence: every connected callback obtains live timing at `JammaLib/src/audio/AudioHost.cpp:437`–`453`, reaching `NinjamConnection::GetLiveTiming`, which calls `_client->GetPosition`, `GetSampleRate`, `GetActualBPM`, and `GetBPI` at `JammaLib/src/ninjam/NinjamConnection.cpp:709`–`724`. The same block calls `_client->GetPosition` again in `ProcessExportBlock` at `:588`–`:590` before the required `_client->AudioProc` at `:698`–`:704`. The bundled opaque library has no inspectable source, and its public contract says “call AudioProc, (and only AudioProc) from your audio thread” at `lib/njclient/njclient.h:171`–`172`. Each `GetLiveTiming`, `ProcessExportBlock`, and per-remote-station `ConsumeStereoPair` also constructs a `NinjamConnectionUse` (`JammaLib/src/ninjam/NinjamSession.cpp:485`–`517`), whose acquisition is an unbounded retry loop with acquire/release atomic RMWs at `:362`–`:380`. `GetLiveTiming` was introduced into the callback by `f36bfe7`; the getter surface by `d0d208e`; callback-side latency `GetPosition` by `782b8a8`.
- Why it matters: no evidence bounds the latency, locking, allocation, or exception behaviour of getter calls in the binary NJClient library, and its documented thread contract explicitly excludes them. The lease retry is normally short but has no callback-time bound during connection publication churn. A connected block performs at least two leases, plus one per remote station, and two separate position reads.
- Recommended disposition: correct/move. Make the audio boundary invoke only the NJClient operation documented for that thread. Publish the timing fields through an existing fixed-size snapshot/mailbox or obtain them from an explicitly documented audio-safe `AudioProc` result; do not add a class. Acquire one stable connection view for all NINJAM work in a block, or otherwise prove a bounded wait-free lookup. Preserve the exact block-start observation coordinate required by the timing design.
- Protected timing concepts affected: remote timing observation, device-audio observation coordinate, remote master phase, and remote join. The fix must not replace the callback's block-start anchor with a delayed job-thread sample.
- Verification: before refactoring, add (1) a deterministic fake/wrapper test that fails if any NJClient method other than the approved audio operation is invoked under an audio-callback marker and (2) a connection-churn test or instrumented callback harness that proves bounded acquisition and one coherent timing observation per block. Afterward, static call-chain audit plus remote connect/disconnect/reconnect and timing-observation tests; collect callback maximum/percentile duration with several remote stations.
- Human decision: pending.

### S08-03 — Compute the common mapped source coordinate once per block

- Stage / reviewer: Stage 8 — Audio and other hot-path performance; confirms the performance dimension of approved F-006 rather than proposing an independent timing model.
- Scope reviewed / exclusions: repeated callback calculation and traversal only; Stage 9 owns behavioural equivalence and Stage 11 owns integer bounds/rounding.
- Severity: follow-up; should be implemented with F-006's single-owner cleanup, after its prerequisite tests.
- Evidence: while following, every block traverses stations at `JammaLib/src/audio/AudioHost.cpp:456`–`459`; each station traverses its takes at `JammaLib/src/engine/Station.cpp:788`–`795`; every take independently calculates `MapRemoteElapsedToLocal` at `JammaLib/src/engine/LoopTake.cpp:640`–`648`, loads another immutable snapshot, then traverses audio loops and MIDI state at `:649`–`:678`. The common helper performs two 64-bit divisions plus multiplication at `JammaLib/src/ninjam/NinjamLoopAlignment.h:21`–`30`. A command block can additionally traverse the hierarchy to restore before replacement/discipline (`AudioHost.cpp:191`–`214`), apply the command (`:298`–`:302`), begin/rebase (`:355`–`:364`), and then perform the ordinary restore (`:456`–`:459`). Commits `a3f72b4`, `1d665d2`, and `bef7943` established the repeated per-take map. Phase 1 F-006 already identifies `AudioHost` as the common-map owner.
- Why it matters: the common elapsed/source coordinate is identical for every take, yet the callback repeats its most expensive arithmetic and copied-map loads proportional to take count. Per-loop modulo and cursor restoration are necessary; per-take reconstruction of the common ruler is not.
- Recommended disposition: simplify with F-006. Have the `AudioHost`-owned map compute one immutable source coordinate at the block boundary and fan that value through Station to each take; retain per-take MIDI/audio anchors and per-entity modulo. Coalesce command-block traversals where the ordering invariant permits, but do not trade clarity for a speculative micro-optimization.
- Protected timing concepts affected: sync phase map, mapped elapsed time, source/scene coordinate, master phase, per-loop phase, and intentional offsets. The recommendation explicitly retains their distinctions and does not introduce a shared loop cursor.
- Verification: before refactoring, retain or add two focused contracts: (1) unequal audio/MIDI loop lengths with intentional offsets receive the same source coordinate but restore distinct wrapped indices, and (2) replacement/discipline/reconnect/`NoSync` preserve anchors and free-run invalidation. Add test-only instrumentation asserting one common-map calculation per block independent of take count; benchmark callback time versus take count before/after.
- Human decision: pending (deduplicate into F-006 unless integration finds a separate implementation obligation).

### S08-04 — Move the empty-scene timing reset out of the callback

- Stage / reviewer: Stage 8 — Audio and other hot-path performance; consumes and confirms the cost dimension of Stage 7 S07-02.
- Scope reviewed / exclusions: callback work only; S07-02 owns the proven data race and owner correction, while Stages 9/10 own the correct empty-connected behavior and transition state.
- Severity: must fix before merge; integrate with S07-02 rather than assigning a second canonical root cause.
- Evidence: `AudioHost::_OnAudio` synchronously invokes `Scene::OnTick` at `JammaLib/src/audio/AudioHost.cpp:540`–`543`. When total takes becomes zero, `Scene::OnTick` calls `_ClearTimingState(false)` at `JammaLib/src/engine/Scene.cpp:1359`–`1378`; `_ClearTimingState` calls `Quantiser::Clear` and `SetMidiGrain` at `:2271`–`:2275`. Even with `clearTapTempo == false` avoiding `_tapTempoMutex`, `Quantiser::Clear` can release `_masterLoop` and destroy the contents of `_preReclockTakeIds` at `JammaLib/src/engine/Quantiser.cpp:132`–`153`. `SetMidiGrain` then traverses the raw Scene stations and each take, clears the remote grid, copies settings, and may publish MIDI quantisation changes at `:203`–`:232`. Stage 7 additionally proves this path races job-owned state in S07-02.
- Why it matters: releasing the final shared/string/container allocations can invoke deallocation on the device callback, while the traversal is unbounded in station/take count. A rare transition can still cause an audible glitch; acquiring `_sceneMutex` would replace it with a prohibited blocking wait.
- Recommended disposition: move with S07-02. Detect/publish the empty transition with bounded audio-owned state, and let the Scene/job owner clear controller containers and remote MIDI descriptors. If audio-owned Timer/take invalidation must occur at the exact boundary, express only that portion through the existing immutable command/mailbox model. Do not add a class or callback lock.
- Protected timing concepts affected: empty-scene local timing clear, connected remote authority, active/remote MIDI grid, and `NoSync` invalidation. Moving work must preserve the intended difference between empty connected and empty disconnected sessions.
- Verification: before refactoring, add two focused contracts: (1) empty connected session preserves accepted remote timing and (2) removal of the last local take while disconnected clears local timing exactly once. Add a callback-context allocation/deallocation hook asserting the transition performs no container destruction or raw Scene traversal; stress connect/disconnect plus last-take removal.
- Human decision: pending (merge with S07-02 during integration).

## Uncertainties

- NJClient is supplied as a binary library in this worktree. Its getter internals cannot be inspected; the finding relies on the explicit public thread contract and the absence of any bounded-cost guarantee. If upstream provides stronger authoritative documentation, attach it at the gate.
- No profiler or instrumented audio session was run, so likely costs are ranked from frequency, loop structure, opaque calls, allocation/I/O capability, and explicit contracts rather than measured microseconds.
- `ExportLatencyCompensationEnabled` is currently compile-time `false`; delay-line sample loops were reviewed but do not execute in the current build. If enabled later, repeat the hot-path budget review.
- Stage 7 establishes that the immutable snapshot publications are race-free, but neither the C++ standard nor this investigation proves that this platform's `atomic<shared_ptr>` operations are lock-free. The gate should require a target-runtime `is_lock_free`/callback timing check or explicitly accept the existing snapshot implementation's bounded cost; no alternative synchronization scheme is proposed because repository policy prefers this pattern.
- Existing non-timing callback costs such as `Station::OnBounce` copies/searches and generic Trigger/Station console output were observed but deliberately not converted into candidates because the human constrained cleanup to remote timing/sync. They remain visible in the table so the exclusion is auditable.

## Conclusion

All callback functions named by the real-time guide were accounted for, including correction of its three stale/nonexistent names to active equivalents. The branch generally uses fixed mailboxes, preallocated scratch, bounded MIDI work, and allocation-free timing math. Four evidence-backed concerns remain: the NJClient callback-contract/unbounded-acquisition surface (merge with S07-01), callback-side empty reset/deallocation (merge with S07-02), alignment-logging placement required by the human gate, and repeated mapping work that should enrich approved F-006. No direct mutex was found in the reviewed active callback bodies, but the snapshot implementation cost and connection destruction still require explicit gate/Stage 12 treatment.
