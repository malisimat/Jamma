03 /

Held for the whole recording?

This remains a separate rule. A continuous capture-wide hold gets explicit, exact endpoints at 0 and L−1. It does not merge with itself.

Release/replay gestures remain distinct from a key held throughout capture. Quantise cannot move these exact endpoints. The rule survives delayed delivery outside the selected window and native save/load.

04 /

What changed from before.

A wider musical tolerance for captured holds, and a correction to how projected notes keep their endpoints.

Previously

  • Natural unmatched-tail continuation depended on a head exactly at zero.
  • An artificial L−1 capture closure could look like a real key release, splitting a boundary gesture.
  • Release clamping could shorten a translated note. Sorting projected endpoints could make an orphan release end the wrong attack.

Now

  • A captured held tail can connect to the earliest real head within the configured forward window.
  • Synthetic closures retain provenance. A real captured L−1 release stays finite.
  • Source pairs keep their duration through translation and wrapping. Prepared playback and display retain their identities.
settings.loop.midiSeamMergeWindowMs: 200

Non-negative integer milliseconds; values above 200 are allowed. Missing field → default 200. Zero disables positive-distance matching while preserving recorded exact-zero continuation and actual finite overlaps. Converted at the active sample rate, rounded to the nearest sample; ties go upward. Matching searches only the beginning of one loop, even with a larger window. Accepted changes reach existing, new and restored loops through the owner-side format refresh.

Preserved: finite-overlap merging, the general case [3] seam-release policy, source persistence without destructive quantisation, and the explicit full-loop-hold rule. Old files cannot reveal whether an unmarked L−1 off was synthetic, so those endpoints keep their finite-note semantics. Naturally unmatched legacy tails can still use the window.
